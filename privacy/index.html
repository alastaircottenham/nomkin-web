Privacy policy

Last updated 23 August 2026 · Applies to the Nomkin mobile app on iOS and Android, and to nomkin.app

Nomkin keeps everything you log on your phone. There is no account until you ask for one, and if you do, all it does is keep one copy of your data safe so a new phone can pick up where the old one left off. There are no adverts until you switch them on, and if you do, nothing you have logged goes with them. Once a day Nomkin sends one anonymous note about how the app itself was used — counts of taps and screens, with no identifier of any kind in it and nothing you have entered — and Settings has a switch that stops it. Nothing about you is sold, and nothing about you is tracked.

Who this is from

Nomkin is made and published in Australia by an independent developer, who is the data controller for the optional account described below. For anything in this policy, write to hello@nomkin.app.

The quiet copy on your phone

Nomkin also writes a copy of all of the above into its own private storage on the phone, so that your phone's own backup — iCloud Backup on iPhone, Google One backup on Android — carries it, and a reinstall on the same handset picks up where you left off. That copy never comes to us. If it travels at all, it travels inside your device backup, which is governed by Apple's privacy policy or Google's privacy policy and is under your control in your phone's settings.

The optional backup account

This is the one part of Nomkin where the data you have entered leaves your phone, and it only happens if you ask for it. The account is free, it is offered nowhere near the start, nothing in the app is gated behind it, and you can use Nomkin for years without one. It exists for a single purpose: if your phone is lost, broken or replaced, your nomkin comes back.

What you give us to make one

An email address and a password, and nothing else. No name, no date of birth, no phone number. The account is created and held by Google Firebase (Firebase Authentication) acting as our processor. Nomkin never sees your password after you type it; Firebase stores it hashed. Your email address is used for exactly two things — signing you in, and sending you a link if you forget your password or need to confirm the address. There is no mailing list, no newsletter, and nothing to unsubscribe from.

What gets backed up

One copy of everything Nomkin holds on the phone, in the same shape the app's own export produces. That means:

  • your profile — pet name and species, date of birth, sex, height, weight, activity level and goal;
  • every day you have logged, including the day's step total, because the step count is part of the day like everything else on it;
  • the food you logged, with portions, calories and timestamps, and the cached food items behind them;
  • the water you ticked off;
  • your weight check-ins;
  • your coins, your collection, and the record of coins earned or bought — which includes which coin packs you have purchased;
  • your settings and progress counters.

Because that includes food and weight records, which the GDPR treats as special-category health data, sign-up asks for a specific tick that names them rather than a general “accept”. Under the GDPR our legal basis for holding this copy is your explicit consent (Article 6(1)(a) and Article 9(2)(a)), and you can withdraw it at any time by deleting the account.

How and when it is sent

  • Over HTTPS, so it is encrypted in transit, and compressed before it is sent.
  • At most once a day, plus once when you leave the app, plus immediately after you buy coins, plus once when you first create the account. You can also send one at any moment with Back up now.
  • It is stored as one document belonging to your account, replaced whole each time. There is no version history and no trail of old copies — one copy, and that is all.
  • Never over a wipe: if you have deleted your data on the phone, Nomkin will not replace a good copy with the emptiness that replaced it.

Who can reach it, and where it lives

The copy is held in Google Firestore, in a document reachable only by somebody signed in as you. The server-side rules permit each account to read and write its own document and no other. It is encrypted in transit and encrypted at rest by Google. The developer does not read it, and nothing is done with it other than handing it back to you on request.

Firebase is Google's infrastructure, so this copy is stored on Google's servers and may be held or processed outside Australia, including in the United States. Google is bound by its own data processing terms and standard contractual clauses for international transfers, and acts only on our instructions.

Signing out, and deleting

Sign out stops this phone sending any more copies. The stored copy stays, and so does everything on the phone. Delete the account removes the stored copy and the account itself, permanently, and leaves everything on your phone exactly where it is. Both are in Settings → Cloud backup. Full steps are on the delete your account page.

Health and step data

Nomkin asks your phone's health store — Apple Health on iPhone, Health Connect on Android — for one thing: the number of steps taken during the day currently on screen.

  • It reads step counts only. It requests no other category of health or fitness data.
  • It only ever reads. Nomkin never writes anything back to your health store.
  • The step count is used solely to fill the Move bar. It is never used for advertising, marketing, or any use-based data mining, and it is never sold, shared, or disclosed to any third party — including any service provider or data broker.
  • If you have made an account, the day's step total travels inside the backup copy described above, because it is part of your day. It goes nowhere else, and nothing is done with it other than handing it back to you on a new phone.
  • Connecting is entirely optional. Decline, and Nomkin works exactly as before; you top up the Move bar yourself by tapping it.

You can withdraw this permission at any time — in Apple Health under Sources, or in the Health Connect app on Android — and Nomkin will stop reading immediately. On Android, Health Connect also revokes the permission by itself if Nomkin has not read anything for about thirty days. Nothing you have already logged is lost when access ends; the Move bar simply goes quiet until you say yes again.

The camera

If you scan a barcode, Nomkin uses your camera to read the barcode number and nothing else. The barcode is decoded on your device by the operating system's own scanner. No photograph or video is taken, stored, or transmitted, and you can always type the numbers instead — or skip scanning entirely and search or quick-add.

There is one other place a camera can be involved, and Nomkin is not the one asking. If you add a picture to the feedback form, your phone's own picker opens, and on some phones that picker offers to take a new photograph as well as to choose an existing one. Either way the app receives only the single file you chose, and only after you have chosen it.

Looking a food up

Nomkin carries a large slice of the Open Food Facts database — a free, open food database run by a French non-profit — inside the app, so most barcodes are answered on the phone itself with no network at all.

When a barcode or a search is not in there, Nomkin asks Open Food Facts.

  • What travels is the barcode number, or the words you typed, and nothing else. No profile, no calories, no weight, no steps, and no identifier for you or your phone.
  • Open Food Facts will see the network request and therefore your IP address, as any website does. It is not told who you are, and it is not sent anything else.
  • What comes back is cached on your device, so scanning the same item again works with no network at all.

Open Food Facts has its own privacy policy. We do not share any user data with them beyond the search term or barcode described above, and we do not receive any user data from them.

If you buy coins

Optional coin packs are sold through Apple's App Store on iPhone and Google Play on Android. The payment is handled entirely by Apple or Google. Nomkin never sees and never receives your card details, billing address, or store account. It is told which pack was purchased so it can add the coins, and that is the whole of it. That record of which pack you bought is kept on your phone, and travels in the backup copy if you have an account, so a new phone does not start from zero. Those purchases are governed by Apple's privacy policy and Google's privacy policy.

How the app itself is used

Once a day, Nomkin sends one small note about how the app was used. It is the only thing that leaves a phone with no backup account, and it is worth being exact about what it is: counts and coarse bands, about a day that is already over, with no identifier of any kind in it. There is no name, no email, no account, no device identifier and no advertising identifier, and nothing links one day's note to the next one, so two notes from the same phone cannot be told apart from two notes from two phones. Because it only ever describes a finished day, it never says what you are doing right now.

It exists for one reason: without it, there is no way to tell whether the app works for people. Whether the tour helps or gets skipped, whether anybody finds the shop, which way of logging food people actually use. You are told about it on the app's first screen, before anything is counted, and the switch that stops it is in Settings.

This is the whole note. Nothing else is in it, and nothing is added to it without this page changing first.

There is one extra note, sent once, at the end of the very first session. A day's note only goes at the next launch, so somebody who installs Nomkin, skips the tour and never opens it again would otherwise be counted nowhere at all — which is exactly the person the tour question is about. That first note carries less than a daily one: the format number, the app version, iPhone or Android, the language region, whether setting up was finished, and how the tour went. Nothing else, and never again.

What is never in it

  • Nothing you entered. Not your weight, not what you ate, not what anything added up to, not your water, not your targets, not your pet's name, and nothing you typed. The note has no free-text field in it at all.
  • Nothing from your phone's health store. No step count, no step goal, and nothing worked out from either. Data read from Apple Health or Health Connect is never used for analysis of any kind, which is a rule of Apple's and Google's as well as a promise here. Whether health is connected at all is not in the note either.
  • No identifier of any kind. No name, email, account, user ID, device ID, advertising ID, or random identifier of our own. Nothing that could group two notes together, and nothing that could be matched against a backup account.
  • No times. Only which day, never a clock time within it.
  • No location. Nothing reads where you are, and a language setting is not a location.

Where it goes, and who holds it

The note goes to Google Firebase, which acts as our processor, in a project kept separate from the one a backup account goes to. Nothing joins the two. The app can only ever create a note there: it cannot read that store back, cannot change a note and cannot delete one, and those limits are enforced by the server rather than by the app asking nicely.

Firebase is Google's infrastructure, so notes are stored on Google's servers and may be held or processed outside Australia, including in the United States, under the same data processing terms and standard contractual clauses as the backup account. As with any request over the internet — including the one your browser made to read this page — Google's servers see the internet address the note arrives from while it is in transit. Nothing in the note itself records it, and it is not stored alongside one.

Why this is not personal information about you

A note has no identifier in it, carries nothing anybody entered, and cannot be linked to another note, to a backup account, or to a phone. What is left is a count of taps that cannot be attributed to a person, which is anonymous statistical information rather than personal data.

The honest consequence of that is worth saying plainly: because nothing identifies whose a note is, one that has already been sent cannot be found, shown to you, or deleted on request — not by us and not by anybody else, because there is nothing to search by. What you can always do is stop any further ones, in one tap.

Turning it off

Settings → Your data → Your privacy → Share how you use it. Turn it off and nothing about the way you use the app leaves the phone again. Anything already counted but not yet sent is thrown away rather than held back, because a day collected under an agreement you have since withdrawn is not a day that gets to leave. The switch is on when you install Nomkin and the app's first screen says so, and turning it off costs you nothing: no feature behaves differently either way.

If you watch a video for coins

Nomkin can show you a short video from Google in exchange for coins, up to twice a day. It is off until you turn it on, in Settings. Nothing in the app is held back if you never do, you are not nagged about it, and there are no adverts anywhere else in Nomkin: no banners, no interstitials, nothing between you and your day.

Until you turn that switch on, the part of Nomkin that talks to Google has not been started at all. The advertising software is not set running at launch to sit waiting on a flag — the first thing that ever reaches Google's ad network is the video you asked for.

What Google is told

The videos come from Google's AdMob network, through Google's Mobile Ads SDK inside the app. To choose a video, deliver it, and bill the advertiser for it, Google receives:

  • your phone's IP address, as every website you visit receives, from which Google works out an approximate location no finer than a city;
  • technical detail about the device and the app — things like the model, the operating system version, the language, the screen, and which app made the request;
  • what happened to the video — that it was requested, that it was shown, whether it was watched to the end or closed early, and how many you have been shown, so the same one is not put in front of you again.

That is the whole of it. Nothing you have logged goes with it. Not your food, not your calories, not your weight, not your steps, not your profile, not your nomkin, not your coins, and nothing that says who you are. There is no identifier that could tie an ad request to your Nomkin data, because Nomkin does not send one.

The adverts are not aimed at you

  • Every request is made non-personalised — on every request, always, whatever any consent form came back with. Google is not permitted to use profile or interest data to choose what you see. The videos are the same ones for everybody.
  • On iPhone, Nomkin never asks to track you. The App Tracking Transparency prompt is never shown, and cannot be: the app deliberately ships without the tracking usage-description that iOS requires before an app is allowed to ask. The IDFA — the phone's advertising identifier — is therefore never available to it and never read.
  • On Android, the advertising ID is switched off. Nomkin explicitly strips out the advertising-ID permission that Google's own SDK would otherwise add to the app, so the SDK cannot read the Google Advertising ID at all.
  • Nomkin runs no attribution and no revenue reporting per advert, and no third-party measurement of any kind sits around this. Google tells it one thing: whether a video was watched to the end, so it knows whether to pay the coins. Nomkin's own daily note carries how the offer to switch videos on was answered and four counts of how videos ended — never which advert it was, and never anything about who watched it.

Google does still use limited signals of its own to cap how often an advert is repeated, to report to advertisers in aggregate, and to detect fraud and invalid traffic. That is Google's own processing, described in how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services, and for it Google acts as an independent controller rather than on our instructions. Nothing about you comes back to us in return — no report, no profile, no list, not even a count that could be tied to a person.

On iPhone, Nomkin also carries Apple's SKAdNetwork list, which is the privacy-preserving way an advertiser learns that an advert led to an install. It reports in aggregate, with no identifier for the person and nothing for Nomkin to store or see.

Consent, and taking it back

Turning the switch on is your consent, and consent is the legal basis for everything above. In the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, Google's own consent form is shown as well, through Google's User Messaging Platform. If you decline it, or it cannot be completed for any reason, no advert is requested — “we could not ask” is not permission.

The same switch in Settings turns it all off again, for good, in one tap. Coins you have already earned stay exactly where they are. There is a row there too for telling us about a video that was unpleasant, so it can be reported to Google.

Whether the switch is on lives only on this phone. It is not part of the backup copy, so a new phone starts with videos off until you say otherwise. Which of today's two videos have already paid is part of your coin record, and that does travel in the backup, so a restored phone cannot be paid twice for the same day.

If you send feedback

Settings has a Send feedback row with a small form on it: whether it is an idea or something that broke, what you want to say, an email address to reply to, and a picture if you would like to add one. Pressing send is what sends it, and nothing goes before that. The screen names all of this in a sentence directly above the button, so you are not asked to go and find this page first.

What travels is what you typed, your email address, the picture if you added one, and the app's version number and which kind of phone it is on. Nothing from your day goes with it: no food, no calories, no weight, no steps, and nothing from your phone's health store. Those six things are the whole of it, and they are the whole of it by construction rather than by intention — they are listed in one place in the app's source, and three separate tests fail the build if a seventh is ever added.

It is sent over an encrypted connection to a small program at nomkin.app/api/feedback, whose only job is to turn it into an email to the address at the bottom of this page. That program is ours. It passes the message to Resend, an email delivery provider, which acts as our processor and makes no other use of what it carries. We use your address to write back to you and for nothing else: there is no mailing list, and nothing to unsubscribe from.

Nomkin does not have access to your photo library. Adding a picture opens your phone's own picker, which hands the app the single file you chose and nothing else. The app then makes it smaller before it is sent, on your phone. What you attach is entirely your choice, and a screenshot can show whatever was on your screen when you took it, so it is worth a glance before you send one.

We hold your email and what you sent for as long as it takes to answer you and to keep a record of the issue. Write to hello@nomkin.app at any time to have a message and its picture deleted.

Your own email app still works, and it always will. The address is printed on the same screen, next to a button that opens a draft in whatever mail app you already have. That route is there whether or not the form is working, and whether or not you have a connection — if you would rather write to us yourself, nothing about the form is in the way.

If you send us a drawing

This one is about the website rather than the app. nomkin.app/puzzles invites artists to send in a picture to be used as a daily puzzle, through a form. Nothing in the app collects any of this, and the app does not send anything to it: the row inside Nomkin simply opens the page in your own browser, the same way the privacy row does.

If you fill the form in, we receive the title you gave your picture, the name you want shown as the artist, your email address, your drawing, and a record of which version of the submission terms you accepted and when. We use your email address only to talk to you about your submission, and your name only for the credit under the picture if we use it.

The form is run by Tally, which acts as our processor and holds what you send until we collect it. We keep a record of accepted submissions for as long as the picture is in the app, and for a reasonable period afterwards, so that both of us can check what was agreed. Submissions we do not use are deleted once we have decided. Write to hello@nomkin.app at any time to have your submission and your details removed.

You have to be 18 or over to send a drawing, which is set out on the page and in the terms.

Notifications

If you turn reminders on, Nomkin schedules them on your device using the operating system's local notification service. They are composed and delivered on the phone itself. No push server is involved, and no notification token or message content is sent anywhere.

Third parties, and the protection they give your data

Nomkin sells user data to no one. There is no third-party analytics service in the app, no crash reporting, no attribution or marketing SDK, and no tracking library. Counting how the app is used is done by Nomkin's own code, into Nomkin's own store, in the anonymous form set out under how the app itself is used — and nothing there is shared with anybody or used for anything but reading the numbers. Nomkin does not track you across other apps or websites, and it does not use the App Tracking Transparency framework — on iPhone it cannot, because it deliberately carries nothing to ask with.

There is one advertising network, Google AdMob, and it is off until you switch it on. While it is on, it receives an ad request and the technical detail set out above, and nothing you have logged. Because every request is non-personalised and carries no advertising identifier, this is not a sale of personal information, and not a sharing of it for cross-context behavioural advertising, under the United States state privacy laws that use those terms.

The only outside parties involved at all are:

  • Google Firebase — our processor, in two separate projects that are never joined. One holds the sign-in credential and the single backup document, and only if you make an account. The other receives the anonymous daily note, unless you have switched that off. In both, Firebase acts on our instructions and does not use the contents for its own purposes.
  • Google AdMob — only if you switch on videos for coins. Receives the ad request described above, as an independent controller, and nothing you have logged.
  • Open Food Facts — receives a barcode or a search term, and no user data.
  • Apple or Google — process a purchase if you make one, and carry your phone's own device backup if you have that switched on.
  • Resend — our processor, and only if you use the feedback form. Receives what you typed, your email address and any picture you attached, in order to deliver it to us as an email, and makes no other use of it.

There are no parent, subsidiary, or related entities with access to user data. To the extent any of these parties receives anything in connection with Nomkin, they are required to provide the same or equal protection of user data as is stated in this policy and as required by the App Store Review Guidelines. We may also disclose data where the law requires it.

How long things are kept

  • On your phone: for as long as you keep the app. We have no ability to access, retain, or recover any of it.
  • The backup copy, if you made an account: one copy, replaced each time a new one is sent, kept for as long as the account exists. There is no version history and no archive of previous copies.
  • When you delete the account: the copy and the account are removed immediately and permanently — the stored document is deleted first, then the account itself. Neither is retained afterwards, and there is no grace period, no deactivated state, and no way for us to restore it. Residual copies may persist for a short time in Google's own routine system backups before being overwritten in the ordinary course.
  • If you have switched on videos for coins: the switch itself, and the record of which of today's videos have paid, live on your phone with everything else. Whatever Google logs about an ad request is held by Google under its own retention policy, not ours — we hold no copy of it and cannot reach it.
  • The anonymous daily notes: kept for as long as they are useful as statistics. There is no retention period that could be meaningfully offered per person, because nothing in a note says whose it is: they cannot be looked up, singled out or deleted individually by anyone, including us. The counts waiting on your phone to be sent are dropped as soon as the note goes, and dropped unsent if you turn the switch off.
  • Email you send us, and anything sent through the feedback form: kept for as long as it takes to answer and to keep a record of the issue, and deleted on request. A picture attached to a report is kept and deleted with the message it came with. Resend holds a copy in transit under its own retention policy while it delivers.

Deleting your data

There is a short page with the exact steps for deleting your account and the backup copy. In summary, everything is in your hands:

  • Delete an entry: tap any logged item and delete it. Nothing is irreversible in the way that matters, and yesterday can be edited too.
  • Delete everything on the phone: Settings → Your data → Start over, or delete → Delete everything. This permanently empties all Nomkin data from the phone. It does not touch the backup copy, because a delete that took the way back with it would not be much of a safety net.
  • Delete the account and the backup copy: Settings → Cloud backup → Delete the account. This removes the stored copy and the account permanently, and leaves everything on the phone alone.
  • Withdraw a permission: steps, camera and notification permissions can all be withdrawn in your phone's settings at any time, and Nomkin keeps working without them. Videos for coins have their own switch in Settings, and turning it off stops any further ad request at once.
  • Stop the anonymous daily note: Settings → Your data → Your privacy → Share how you use it. Turning it off stops any further note and throws away anything counted but not yet sent. Notes already sent cannot be found or removed, by us or by anyone, because nothing in them says whose they are.
  • Uninstall: removing the app removes everything it holds on the phone along with it. If you have an account, uninstalling does not delete the backup copy — delete the account first if that is what you want.

Your rights

Australian privacy law — the Privacy Act 1988 and the Australian Privacy Principles — gives you rights of access to and correction of personal information held about you, and requires that a policy like this one explain how it is handled. If you are in the United Kingdom or the European Union, the UK GDPR and the EU GDPR give you further rights of erasure, restriction, objection and portability, and the right to complain to your local supervisory authority.

Nomkin is built so that all of those rights are exercised directly, by you, on your own device: everything it holds is visible in the app, editable in the app, and deletable in one tap. If you have an account, the backup copy is a copy of that same data — you can bring it back to a phone whenever you like, which is portability, and delete it whenever you like, which is erasure, both without asking anyone. If you would rather we did it, or you cannot sign in, write to hello@nomkin.app and we will act on it. For anything you have never uploaded to an account, there is nothing for us to act on, because we hold no copy of it.

The anonymous daily note is the one thing these rights have no grip on, and not by our choice: rights of access, correction, erasure and portability all work by finding the data that is about you, and a note carries nothing that could find it. That is the trade the design makes. It gives up ever being able to answer a request about a note, in exchange for there being nothing in one to answer about, and it leaves you the control that does work — a switch that stops it, with no reason to give and nobody to ask.

If you are in Australia and are unhappy with how we have handled a privacy matter, you may complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

Children

Nomkin is not directed at children under 13, and the backup account is not offered to them: you must be at least 13 to create one. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children. Videos for coins fall under the same line: they are an opt-in feature of an app that is not for under-13s, and because every request is non-personalised and carries no advertising identifier, nothing about whoever watches one is profiled in any case. If you believe a child has made an account, write to hello@nomkin.app and it will be deleted.
