Puzzle submission terms

Version 1 · 23 August 2026 · For artwork sent through the form at nomkin.app/puzzles

The short version: you keep your drawing. You are lending it to Nomkin, not selling it or giving it away, and you can ask for it back out whenever you like. In return we credit you by name, we do not pay you, and we cannot promise that every drawing will be used.

These terms are the agreement between you and us about a drawing you send us. They are written plainly on purpose. If something here does not sit right with you, please just don't send anything, and no hard feelings.

Nomkin is made and published in Australia by an independent developer (“we”, “us”). By sending us a drawing through the form you agree to these terms as they read on the day you sent it. The version number and date at the top of this page are what you are agreeing to, and the form records which version that was.

These are separate from the terms of use for the app itself, which still apply to you as somebody using Nomkin.

You must be 18 or over

You have to be at least 18 to send us a drawing. This is not us being unfriendly about younger artists, and it is not about the app, which anybody 13 or over can use. It is because these terms are an agreement, and an agreement made with somebody under 18 is not one that either of us could rely on later. We would also rather not publish a young person's name next to their work, or hold their email address.

If you are under 18, please keep drawing. We would genuinely love to see your work when you are old enough to send it.

If any of that turns out not to be true, we will take the picture out of the next update, and you agree to cover us for any claim that comes from our having used it.

The drawing stays yours. We are not asking you to sign it over, and we are not asking for exclusivity. You can sell it, print it, post it, license it to somebody else or put it in your portfolio, and you never need to ask us about any of that.

Where your picture appears, and money

We would rather be straight with you about this than leave it in the small print.

  • Nomkin is a free app that makes money. It sells an in‑app currency (coins), and it can show rewarded video adverts to people who have deliberately switched them on.
  • Your picture sits on a screen where those adverts are offered. The daily puzzle is cut into five pieces, and each piece is turned over by one of five things a player can do that day. Three of the five are ordinary things like logging a meal or reaching a step goal. Two of them are optional rewarded videos. So a player who wants to see the whole of your drawing on the day it shows may choose to watch two adverts to get there.
  • You are not paid, and you get no share of any revenue from coins, adverts, or anything else. There is no fee, no royalty and no payment of any kind.
  • We may still make money while your drawing is in the app. You are giving us permission to use it in a commercial product knowing that, and that is what you are agreeing to when you tick the box on the form.

If that is not a trade you want to make, please don't send us anything. It is a completely reasonable thing to decide.

Credit

If we use your drawing, we credit you. Your name appears under the picture on the day it shows, and with the picture in a player's collection, exactly as you wrote it on the form. Tell us the name you want people to see, whether that is your full name, a first name or a nickname, and that is the one we use.

Write to us if you want the credit changed later, or removed, and we will change it in the next update.

What we do to the file

A puzzle picture has to work as a puzzle, so the app does a few things to it, and we need your consent for them. By sending us a drawing you agree that we may:

  • resize it and convert it to another image format;
  • crop or adjust it slightly so that it fits the frame;
  • cut it into five pieces on screen, show some of those pieces face down, and animate them turning over;
  • show it at different sizes, and rotated a quarter turn when a phone is held upright;
  • shorten the title if it does not fit.

For anybody who knows the term: this is your consent, under Part IX of the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth), to acts that would otherwise infringe your moral right of integrity. Your right to be named as the artist is not affected, and we intend to honour it. We will not change the drawing itself, beyond what is listed above, without asking you.

Taking it back out

You can change your mind at any time, for any reason, without explaining it. Write to hello@nomkin.app and we will remove your picture from the next update of the app.

Two honest limits on that. We cannot reach into an app that is already installed, so copies already on people's phones keep working until they update. And a player who has already collected the picture may still have it in their collection on their own device. What we can promise is that it goes out of every new version once you have asked.

We might not use it

Every submission is looked at by a person, and we cannot use them all. A drawing might not suit the app's style, might not work once it is cut into five, or might simply arrive when we already have plenty. None of that is a judgement on you or on your work.

We are not able to give feedback on individual submissions, and we cannot promise a reply either way. Sending us a drawing does not oblige us to use it, and we can remove a picture we have used at any time, without giving a reason.

If we do use it, it will appear in a future update rather than straight away, because the pictures are built into the app itself.

The form asks for your name, your email address and your drawing. We use your email address only to talk to you about your submission. Your name is used for the credit if we use your picture. We keep a record of accepted submissions, including which version of these terms you agreed to, so that both of us can check later what was agreed.

The form itself is run by Tally, which holds what you send until we collect it. What happens to your details is set out in the privacy policy.
